OmniReach: the user guide

How to run outbound voice campaigns on the platform, from signing in to reading the outcome back out. Written for whoever is actually driving the console, not for whoever installed it. If you want to know how the machinery works underneath, that is a different document (HOW-OMNIREACH-WORKS.html).

OmniReach dials a list of people, has a real conversation with each of them using an AI voice, takes actions during that conversation (books a callback, records a promise to pay, notes which documents are still missing), and pushes the outcome back to wherever you keep your records.

The whole of it is driven by one thing: the company profile. Choose an industry and a country, and the console assembles a complete agent from it, including the brand name, the voice, the language, the currency, how it addresses people, the compliance framework it works under, and the conversation playbooks it is allowed to run. Change the profile and it is a different company in a different market, with no code touched. Almost everything in this guide follows from that.

1. Signing in

There is no password to remember. Put your work email in, and a six-digit code arrives in your inbox within a few seconds. Type it, and you are in. The code lasts ten minutes and works once.

We built it this way on purpose. Passwords for a demo platform get written on whiteboards and shared in group chats, and then nobody knows who actually placed a call. A code sent to a mailbox proves the mailbox, every time, without anyone having to invent a password they will forget.

Your browser is remembered after the first sign-in, so you will not be asked for a code every morning. Signing out from the profile menu (top right, your initial) clears that.

If you have never signed in before

Create an account from the sign-in screen. What happens next depends on your email domain:

Your domainWhat happens
On the approved listYou register, get a code, and you are in. Nobody at our end has to do anything.
Approved individually, by addressSame, and it works even when open sign-up is switched off, because somebody already said yes to you specifically.
Anything elseYou are told plainly that the request has gone for review, and it appears in our Access requests queue. Nothing on the platform is reachable until it is approved.
Being refused is not a fault. If you get "sent to an administrator for review", the platform is working correctly. Send a note to your Streebo contact and it takes about a minute to approve at our end.

2. Set the company first

Before you do anything else, look at the top of the screen. There is a pill that reads operating as followed by a company name. That is the agent that will make your calls, and everything downstream is shaped by it.

To change it, go to Company Profile. The fastest route is to load one of the industry presets, then edit the two or three fields that matter for your prospect: the company name, the country, the agent's own name. That is usually enough for a demo.

The profile decides:

  • Which call types you can run. Only the playbooks enabled in the active profile appear on the Single Call screen. A clinic profile offers appointment confirmations and results; it does not offer loan collections, because a clinic does not make those calls.
  • What the agent sounds like. Voice, language, and how it addresses people (first name, "Mr / Ms" plus surname, first name plus "ji", "sir / ma'am").
  • The money and the dates. Currency word, and whether amounts are grouped the Indian way (25,00,000) or the Western way (2,500,000). The agent speaks amounts as words, so this matters more than it looks.
  • The compliance framework it works under, which changes what it will and will not say.
Change the profile before the campaign, not during. A campaign takes a copy of the profile at the moment you launch it and uses that copy for every call in the run. This is deliberate: a campaign that changed identity halfway through would be much harder to explain than one that finishes as it started. If you edit the profile mid-run, the change applies to your next campaign.

3. Simulation and live

Top right of every screen there is a SIMULATION switch. It is the single most important control on the platform, so it is worth being precise about what it does.

Simulation onLive
A phone ringsNoYes
It costs moneyNoYes, telephony and voice minutes
You get a full transcriptYesYes
Outcome, disposition, sentimentYesYes
Analytics and write-back fill inYesYes
Counts against your daily allowanceNoYes

A simulated call runs the same conversation logic and produces the same shaped record, including the actions the agent would have taken. Every simulated record is stamped as such, shows a Simulated marker in the history, and is counted separately everywhere it matters. It never quietly inflates a real number.

Use simulation for everything except the moment you want a real phone to ring. Building a profile, checking a script reads well, testing a CSV, showing a prospect the analysis table, proving the write-back columns: none of that needs telephony. Most of a demo can be run in simulation, and often should be.

4. One call

Single Call is the screen to use when you want to hear the thing work, or when you are demonstrating one specific conversation.

  1. Pick a use case. These are the playbooks enabled in the active profile. Each one is a whole conversation, not a template sentence.
  2. Fill in the customer. Name and phone number. The number must be in international format, starting with a plus and the country code: +919820011001, not 9820011001. Time of day is filled in for you from the clock.
  3. Fill in the call variables. These change with the use case. Required ones are marked with an asterisk. A bill reminder wants the product, the amount and the due date; an appointment confirmation wants the type, the date and the time.
  4. Launch.
Leave a variable blank rather than inventing something. The agent is instructed to speak around an empty variable gracefully, so an unknown balance simply never comes up. Putting a made-up number in is worse than leaving it out, because the agent will say it with complete confidence to a real person.

5. Calling a list

Smart Targeting is where a file becomes a queue of calls. Upload the export as it comes out of your system. You do not need to rename the columns.

What the file needs

A name and a phone number, somewhere in it. That is the whole requirement. Everything else shapes which call is placed and what gets said on it, but a row without a name or a number cannot be called at all and will be held back.

The columns are matched three ways, in this order:

  1. By name. due_date, and the several hundred aliases people actually use in the wild: msisdn, consignee, appt_dt, guardian_phone, Bill Amount, Payment Due On.
  2. By meaning, when the header is longer but still says what it is: bill_due_date, restoration_eta, pending_documents.
  3. By the values themselves, when the header says nothing useful. A column called crm_field_7 full of +9198… is the phone number, and one full of people's names is the name.

Semicolon files (which is what Excel produces across most of Europe and Latin America), tab separated files, a byte-order mark, a report with its own title on the first line, headers with units in them such as Amount (INR): all handled. So are the things Excel does to your data, like turning a long phone number into 9.19820011001E+11 or writing a date as the number 46272.

Two sample files

Both are on the Smart Targeting screen.

  • Download CRM Template is the tidy version, using our own column names. Useful as a starting point when someone asks what the file should look like.
  • Download Sample Export is the interesting one. It is deliberately shaped like a partner's own export: their column names, no use-case column, gaps in it, and several different kinds of call sitting in the same upload. Upload it unchanged and every row still finds the right call. In a demo, this is the file to use.

Other places the data can come from

Under Data source you can also pull from a URL (CSV or JSON), a published Google Sheet, or a REST API. Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho and a direct database connection are wired up to the same interface but need credentials before they will run.

6. Reading the analysis

When the file is analysed you get two things: a summary of how your columns were understood, and a row-by-row table of what will happen.

"How your file was read"

A list of pairs, Mobile No → Phone number, Account Holder → Customer name. Check this first. Everything below it is downstream of this mapping, and if we have read the wrong column as the due date, this panel is the only place you will see it before a call goes out. Any column we did not use is listed at the end.

The table

ColumnWhat to look at
OrderThe dialling order. Urgent calls go first: a service disruption, then arrears, then a payment reminder, then appointments, then documents, renewals, feedback, leads, and marketing last.
Use CaseThe call this row will get.
ConfidenceHow sure the routing is, and what it would otherwise have chosen. "Named in file" means your file told us outright, using a use_case column.
Why this callThe evidence, in words: "payment due in 5 days", "23 days overdue", "documents outstanding". Worth reading aloud in a demo.
What we'll sayThe actual values the agent will speak for that person. This is the fastest way to prove the data was really understood.
StatusReady, or the reason it was held back.

Why rows get skipped

ReasonWhat it means
no phone number / no customer nameExactly that. It names which one is missing rather than saying "missing data" and leaving you to hunt.
missing Deadline (or another field)The row wants a particular call but does not carry something that call cannot do without. The missing column is named, so you can add it and re-upload.
do-not-call (data)A suppression column in your file. Spelled however you like: Opted Out, DNC, Do Not Contact, unsubscribed, Consent Revoked, and others. We match this generously on purpose, because the cost of missing it is a call to somebody who asked us not to ring.
do-not-call (prior call)That number asked us to stop on an earlier call. It is honoured across campaigns.
duplicate numberThe same number appears earlier in the file. We call it once.

You may also see a row that says nothing on this row matches the calls this agent makes. That is not a failure to read the file. It means the profile genuinely has no call for what the row describes, which happens when, for instance, a file full of amounts and due dates is uploaded against a clinic profile that only books appointments. Change the profile, or the file.

The one warning worth stopping for. If every date in a column falls on or before the 12th, nothing in the file says whether it is written day-first or month-first, and you will get an amber note saying so. We read it day-first, so 09/11 is the 9th of November. If the export came out of an American system it means the 11th of September and every date on those rows is wrong. A single date past the 12th anywhere in that column settles it and the warning disappears.

7. Launching the campaign

Move to Bulk Campaign. You will see the queued count, the breakdown by call type, and a delay setting between calls (three seconds by default). Name the campaign if you want it to be findable later, then launch.

Progress updates live. The Stop button takes effect between calls, so a call already connected will finish its conversation properly rather than being cut off mid-sentence.

Simulated campaigns run fast on purpose. The delay between calls is shortened to a fraction of a second when simulating, so a two hundred row file finishes while you are still talking about it.

8. Call History

Every call, with its transcript and its recording. Three tabs across the top:

  • Personal, your own calls. This is the default, because it is what you usually want.
  • Colleagues, other people in your organisation.
  • Company, everything your organisation has done.

Open a call and you get the transcript, the recording where there is one, and an Actions captured block: the disposition, a one-sentence summary, and whatever the agent recorded during the conversation. A promise to pay with the amount and date. A rescheduled appointment with the new time. Which documents are still outstanding and when the customer said they would send them. Whether a service disruption was understood and which option they chose. A dispute, a transfer, a corrected phone number.

If the recording is not there yet, wait a moment. The transcript, summary and outcome arrive by webhook from ElevenLabs when the call ends, which is normally seconds after the customer hangs up, not instantly. A call that has just finished can briefly show as ended with nothing attached. Refresh and it will be there.

9. Write-back

A call that nobody records is a call that has to be listened to again later. Write-back pushes the outcome of every finished call to wherever you keep your records.

Choose a destination:

  • Preview in-app, which writes nowhere and simply shows you the rows. Start here.
  • Webhook, a POST of JSON to any URL you give it, with optional headers for authentication.
  • Enriched CSV, downloadable.
  • Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho and SQL are built against the same interface and need credentials.

What ends up in a row

A stable core is always present: who was called, on what number, which call it was, the outcome, a summary, the sentiment, when, and the call id. Beyond that, a row carries whatever that particular call produced and nothing else, so a documents chase does not arrive padded out with forty empty payment columns.

There is one column worth pointing out in a demo, because it is the one a human actually reads: next_step. It says in plain words what happens now, drawn from whichever outcome the call produced.

next_step
"Pay 12500 by Friday"
"Booked for Tuesday at 10:30"
"Documents by Friday"
"Blocked: passport is with the visa office"
"Transferred to collections"
"Do not call again"

Alongside that you get everything the agent captured (promise amount and date, dispute, transfer, follow-up messages sent, corrected contact details, survey score and verbatim, offer decision and the reason for it, documents outstanding) and the fields extracted automatically from the conversation after it ends, including the objection the customer raised and which language the call actually ran in.

If you would rather control the columns yourself, set a custom mapping. A column you ask for is always written, whether or not that call filled it, because you asked for it.

10. Analytics

How many calls, to which countries, how long, how they ended, and what the agent achieved. Same Personal / Colleagues / Company tabs as the history, and the same default.

Simulated calls are excluded by default. There is a switch to include them, which is the right thing to use when you want to show a full-looking dashboard without having spent anything on telephony. Turn it on knowingly, and turn it off before quoting a number to anyone.

11. Scheduler

Calls and campaigns that run without anybody at a keyboard. Each schedule carries its own agent, its own market, its own timezone and its own calling window, so several can be running against different countries at once without interfering with each other.

A schedule takes a snapshot of the agent profile when you create it. That is intentional, and the same reasoning as campaigns: a schedule that quietly changed identity because somebody edited a profile last Tuesday would be an unpleasant surprise on a Monday morning.

Schedules are visible to your whole organisation so that a colleague covering for you can see what is queued. Pausing or deleting one stays with whoever created it.

12. Agent Builder

Compose an agent for an industry and a region from scratch, then save it. Useful when you are preparing for a specific prospect and want it waiting for you next time rather than rebuilt from memory.

Saved configurations are visible across your organisation, so the team works from one library rather than each person rebuilding the same prospect. Editing and deleting stay with whoever saved it: seeing a colleague's work and being able to overwrite it are different things.

13. Company Profile

The full detail behind section 2. Load a preset, then edit. The fields that change the call most noticeably are the company name, the agent's name, the country and language, the honorific style, and which use cases are switched on.

Further down there is a knowledge section: a few plain facts the agent can draw on, one per line, written the way you want them spoken. Numbers and codes are read aloud, so write them as you would say them rather than as you would type them.

14. Your allowance

Accounts have a daily ceiling on live calling. The standard allowance is three calls or ten minutes of talk time per day, whichever runs out first. Simulated calls do not count against it.

Bulk campaigns are switched off for partner accounts by default. If you try to launch one you will see this:

Bulk campaigns aren't switched on for your account just yet. Your daily allowance is 3 calls or 10 minutes of talk time, so calls are placed one at a time from the Single Call page. If you'd like to run a larger campaign, just ask your Streebo contact and we'll be glad to arrange it.

This is not an oversight. Somebody on a three-call allowance who uploads five hundred contacts gets a queue of refusals rather than a demo, and if the allowance were raised without the ceiling being thought about first, one careless upload becomes real money and five hundred real phones ringing. So bulk is handed over deliberately, per account, and it can be granted for a day or a week rather than for ever.

Ask, and it takes a minute. Allowances and bulk access can be raised temporarily for a specific demo, with an expiry date attached, so nobody has to remember to turn it back down.

15. Admin administrators only

The Admin tab appears only for administrators, and the restricted parts of it are not sent to a browser that has no business with them. If you are not an administrator, the tab is not hidden from you, it is simply not there.

What lives in it:

  • Accounts. Create, deactivate, set an allowance, grant a temporary boost including bulk access.
  • Access requests. People from unapproved domains who have asked to get in. Approving one puts them into an organisation you choose.
  • Sign-up and limits. The approved domains, individually approved addresses, and the two switches that govern self-registration. There is a live summary that states in plain words what the current combination actually does, so you are not left to infer it from two checkboxes.
  • Guardrails. The default daily allowance, whether it is enforced, whether the whole platform is restricted to simulation, and whether partners may run bulk campaigns.
  • Daily report. A summary emailed to Streebo once a day covering every partner: calls placed, who was called and in which country, talk time, industries demonstrated, and any boosts in force. The time, timezone and recipients are all editable.

16. When something looks wrong

"Backend not connected" across the top

The console cannot reach the server. On a hosted instance that usually means the service is down or restarting; give it a moment and press Retry. Running locally, it means the Node server on port 3002 is not up.

Every row in my file was skipped

Look at the "How your file was read" panel first. If the name or the phone number is not listed there, we did not find them, and no row can be called without both. If they are listed and rows are still held back, the Status column names the missing field per row.

The agent did not mention the amount

That variable was empty for that row, and the agent is instructed to speak around an empty variable rather than say a blank or read out a placeholder. Check the "What we'll say" column in the analysis: it shows exactly what the agent was given.

A date was spoken as the wrong day

Almost always the day-first / month-first question. See the amber note in section 6. Add one date past the 12th to that column, or write dates as 2026-09-11, and it is unambiguous.

The call ended sooner than expected

Live calls are capped at whatever talk time your account has left for the day, so the last call of an allowance can be cut short. The cap exists so a single long call cannot overspend a ten-minute budget.

A number I already called is being skipped

If it was marked do-not-call on an earlier conversation, that is honoured permanently, across every future campaign. It is shown as do-not-call (prior call) rather than silently dropped.

Analytics look emptier than the history

Simulated calls are left out of analytics unless you switch them on. The history shows everything.

17. What it will not do

Worth knowing before somebody asks you in a meeting.

  • It does not take inbound calls. This is an outbound platform. A customer cannot ring the number back and reach the agent.
  • It does not check whether a number is reachable before dialling. A dead number is discovered by calling it, and comes back as no answer.
  • It does not retry. A call that goes to voicemail is recorded as such and left. Re-dialling is a decision for a person, or a second campaign.
  • It does not translate your data. The agent speaks the language in the profile. If your file has French product names and the profile is English, it will read French product names in English.
  • Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho and direct database write-back need credentials. The interface is built and the mapping works; the connection is not something you can switch on from the console alone.
  • It cannot tell 09/11 apart from 11/09 in a column where no date passes the 12th. Nothing can. We say so rather than guessing quietly.
  • It will not invent a value to fill a gap. If an amount or a date is missing, the call either works around it or is held back with the missing column named. This is a deliberate limit, and the right one: a specific number spoken to a real customer has to be theirs.

18. Words we use

TermMeans
ProfileThe company the agent is being right now. Brand, market, voice, language, compliance, and which call types are available.
Use caseOne kind of call, named the way that industry names it: "EMI Due Reminder", "Delivery Exception", "e-KYC Pending".
